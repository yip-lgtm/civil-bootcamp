# Module 1 — Equilibrium, load path, truss, SFD/BMD
Corresponds to Vol.1 Ch.1–4 | MIT 1.050 / 1.036

If you cannot narrate the path, you do not have a structure — you have a drawing.

## Q1. Five core mental models every expert shares

1. A support is permitted motion, not a mood. Draw allowed rigid-body motion before reactions.
2. Equilibrium is necessary, not sufficient. After a small motion, restoring actions must appear.
3. Every load case needs a continuous story. Construction-stage gravity is a different story from the finished building.
4. Stiffness attracts force. A weak or missing path is not a path.
5. SFD/BMD must obey dV/dx = -w and dM/dx = V. If the sketch violates the calculus, the figure is false.

## Q2. Three disagreements and strongest arguments

1. Ideal pin/roller vs finite-stiffness bearings.
   Ideal: first see mechanism. Stiffness: real steel pins and bridge bearings redistribute.
2. Transfer to resolve discontinuity vs land vertical members whenever possible.
   Transfer: architecture is a real constraint. Landing: every turn creates punching and shear concentration.
3. Elastic BMD vs plastic-redistribution envelope.
   Elastic: one clear picture. Envelope: patterned live load will demand other sections.

## Q3. Ten depth questions (answers)

Q1. Robustness asks about the remaining system. A statically determinate beam becomes a mechanism if one support vanishes; a continuous beam may not.
Q2. An internal hinge releases rotation, not vertical force. V is fixed by vertical equilibrium.
Q3. Three concurrent bars give zero restoring lever arm under a tiny motion — instantaneous mechanism. Counting said enough; geometry said no.
Q4. One-way vs two-way splits on span ratio and reinforcement, not on arrows drawn by habit.
Q5. Danger is at the turn: punching under the floating column, and shear in the transfer beam just inside the landing columns.
Q6. A core that cannot be reached through a cut diaphragm is not a storey-level path.
Q7. Zero-force member rules fail if the joint has an external load, members are not colinear, or another load case / erection stage uses that bar.
Q8. UDL requires parabolic M. A sharp peak belongs to a point load. The sketch then contradicts dV/dx = -w.
Q9. V = 0 at max M is an interior stationary-point rule. A cantilever peak lives at the boundary.
Q10. Invert the BMD: straight = no distributed load; parabola = UDL; kink = point load; jump in M = couple. Then check reactions.

Worked check: a transfer beam during pour has no floating column yet. Draw today's path, not the completed one.
